This simulator teaches the direction and the approximate size of each effect on image quality, patient dose and staff dose in fluoroscopy and interventional radiology. It is not a guide for clinical protocols or for setting up equipment. Any change to clinical settings must be reviewed by a team: radiologist or interventionalist, radiographer, medical physicist and, where needed, the manufacturer's application specialist. The author accepts no responsibility for harm or damage resulting from protocol changes.

Live images

Press Run, or hold the space bar like a foot pedal. While the beam is on, both monitors expose at their own settings and add up their own fluoroscopy time, Ka,r, KAP and skin dose; when it is off they show the last image hold (LIH). The dose clock speeds up the counters only. A tone sounds while the beam is on (pulsing in high-dose mode, harsher in acquisition) and an alarm sounds every 5 minutes of fluoroscopy, as required by regulation: click the image to reset it.

What the last change did, and why

Definitions used in this lab

  • Equipment: R/F table with the tube under the table (detector above the patient); remote R/F table with the tube over the table (operator usually in the control room); mobile surgical C-arm (fixed SID 100 cm, low power, image intensifier or flat panel); fixed interventional C-arm (high power, isocentre 74 cm from the focus, spectral Cu filtration, ceiling shield and table curtain).
  • Spectrum and attenuation: the same validated model as the radiography lab (tungsten anode, HVL within ±0.15 mm Al of published values), with Cu filtration, the table (≈ 1.5 mm Al) before or after the patient, and NIST coefficients for tissue, bone, iodine and steel.
  • ABC (automatic dose-rate control): holds the detector dose per frame at a target (low 15, normal 30, high 60 nGy for a 25 cm flat-panel field; acquisition 150 nGy; DSA 3 µGy). The target rises when the field is smaller (×FOV−1 for flat panels, ×FOV−2 for image intensifiers) and, at more than 15 pulses/s, falls per frame. Each curve chooses the lowest kV at which the needed mA stays below a fraction of the tube limit. The air-kerma rate at the reference point is capped at 88 mGy/min (176 in high-level mode).
  • Reference point (Ka,r): interventional C-arm 15 cm from the isocentre towards the tube (IEC 60601-2-43); mobile C-arm 30 cm from the image receptor; under-table R/F 1 cm above the table; over-table R/F 30 cm above the table. Ka,r is not the skin dose: the skin can be closer or further, and the table and backscatter change the skin dose.
  • Image quality: SNR per pixel from the noise-equivalent quanta in one frame (with flat-panel electronic noise and 2 × 2 binning for large fields), contrast after scatter (and veiling glare for image intensifiers), static MTF, and d′ for the task. The perceived d′ adds temporal integration: about 0.2 s of frames by the eye, extended by the recursive filter. Motion blur = speed × pulse width; lag = filter time constant.
  • Patient dose: skin dose rate = Ka at the skin × table transmission × backscatter factor; KAP = air kerma × field area. Peak skin dose = skin dose rate × fluoroscopy time + dose per acquisition frame × frames, × 1.06 (tissue/air), assuming one projection. Effective dose ≈ KAP × coefficient (abdomen 0.26, heart 0.20, hip 0.29, leg 0.01 mSv per Gy·cm²), adjusted for HVL.
  • Staff dose: scatter at 1 m ≈ 2 µGy per Gy·cm² of KAP, higher on the tube side (eye level for tube over the table, legs for tube under the table), falling with the square of distance. Lead apron transmission from the Archer equation with the NCRP 147 lead data (for example 2.7% for 0.35 mm Pb at 80 kV; scatter is softer, so the real value is a little lower), ceiling shield 90%, lead glasses 60%, table curtain 88% reduction. TLD reading Hp(10) = air kerma × Hp(10)/Ka (ICRU 57) at the mean energy of 90° scatter: collar TLD over the apron at neck height, trunk TLD under the apron at waist height. Effective dose under the apron: E = 0.5 HW + 0.025 HN (NCRP 122). Annual eye lens limit 20 mSv.
  • Real units (QC data): 19 fluoroscopy units measured in routine quality control (facility names removed). Some factors were not measured and are estimated; these are listed under the QC data of each unit. Acquisition doses are calibrated only where a dose per frame was measured. Units differ from one installation and software version to another: treat these numbers as examples, not as values for your own unit.
  • Low-contrast discs: 18 discs of 11 mm with nominal contrast 16.7% to 0.9%, as in the Leeds TOR 18FG phantom. Contrast is scaled with the attenuation of aluminium at the mean beam energy and reduced by scatter and veiling glare. A disc counts as visible when d′ ≥ 3 for a human observer looking at live fluoroscopy (efficiency 3%, calibrated so that normal-mode fluoroscopy shows about 10–12 discs).
  • Generic equipment: values from the physics model only. Real units: the same model, scaled so that at the quality-control reference conditions (normal level, average patient = 2 mm Cu phantom, largest field, preset pulse rate, reference SID) it gives the measured entrance dose rate; dose level, pulse rate, patient size and zoom then follow the measured factors, the kV follows the measured technique, and the unit's own maximum dose rate is applied. Geometry, collimation, Cu filtration, grid, angulation and image processing still follow the physics model. Set your local DRL in the comparison panel.
